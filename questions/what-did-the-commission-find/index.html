The 115 charges, explained

What did the Commission find?

On 29 September 2026 an Independent Commission found Manchester City guilty of serious breaches of the Premier League's financial rules across nine seasons. Here is what was alleged, what was found, and what the club said, from the official documents only.

Updated 27 October 2026. Official sources only.

What are the 115 charges?

On 6 February 2023 the Premier League referred alleged breaches by Manchester City to an independent Commission. The referral grouped them into five categories, each broken down by season and by rule. “115” is the number most often used for the total.

  • Financial information (2009/10 to 2017/18): failing to provide accurate information giving a true and fair view of the club's finances, including revenue such as sponsorship, related parties and operating costs.
  • Pay disclosure: not including full manager pay (2009/10 to 2012/13) and player pay (2010/11 to 2015/16) in the relevant contracts.
  • UEFA rules (2013/14 to 2017/18): Club Licensing and Financial Fair Play.
  • Profitability and Sustainability (2015/16 to 2017/18): the Premier League's spending rules.
  • Co-operation (December 2018 onwards): failing to co-operate with the Premier League's investigation.

Source: Original referral to the Commission (Premier League · 6 Feb 2023)

What did the Commission decide?

According to the Premier League's statement, the Commission found City guilty of all charges of serious breaches of its financial rules over the nine seasons from 2009/10 to 2017/18, and upheld three of the four alleged co-operation breaches.

The statement says the club used sham commercial contracts with sponsors and partners to inflate revenue and reduce costs, with the difference funded by its owner, Abu Dhabi United Group; filed misstated accounts; and was significantly in breach of Premier League and UEFA spending limits. It puts the effect at more than £900 million, and quotes the Commission's conclusion that the club “clearly intended to circumvent the PL Rules”.

Source: Premier League statement on the decision (Premier League · 29 Sep 2026) · Independent Commission: Redacted Core Decision (PDF · 29 Sep 2026)

What did Manchester City say?

In a statement the same day, the club said it was “disappointed and surprised”, that it is innocent of the accusations, and that the decision contains errors of law, principle and fact. It said it would pursue the appeal routes open to it.

Source: Manchester City club statement (Man City · 29 Sep 2026)

How long did it take?

The Premier League says it opened its investigation in December 2018, issued the complaint in February 2023, and the 42-day hearing finished in December 2024. The decision was published on 29 September 2026.

Source: Premier League statement on the decision (Premier League · 29 Sep 2026)
